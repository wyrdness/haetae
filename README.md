## Overview

The Haetae (Korean: 해태; Hanja: 獬豸), known in Chinese as Xiezhi (xièzhì), is a mythological creature shared across East Asian traditions that has taken on particular cultural importance in Korea. In Korean tradition the creature is described as having a muscular leonine body covered with sharp scales, a bell at its neck, and a single long horn on its forehead. The creature is believed to possess great intellect and the innate ability to distinguish right from wrong; according to legend, it would ram the guilty with its horn while sparing the innocent. This judicial quality made the haetae an enduring symbol of law and justice across the region. Stone statues of the haetae guard the gates of Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul, where they were placed during the Joseon dynasty to ward off fire disasters. In China, law enforcement officials wore xiezhi crowns from the Qin and Han dynasties onward, and Tang-dynasty censors wore xiezhi hats during impeachment proceedings. The creature continues in modern use: the Haechi cartoon character serves as the official mascot of Seoul, and the People's Republic of China engraves xiezhi imagery on court gavels. In 2022, the prehistoric giraffoid species Discokeryx xiezhi was scientifically named in reference to the creature's distinctive horn.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: The Korean name 해태 (haetae) is a Korean reading of the Chinese characters 獬豸. Linguist Axel Schuessler reconstructs the Late Han pronunciation as *gɛʔ-ḍɛʔ* and proposes an Austroasiatic etymology related to words for deer or stag in Old Mon, Old Khmer, and Burmese.
- **Meaning**: The characters 獬豸 refer to a mythical beast that distinguishes guilt from innocence. The English descriptive name 'unicorn-lion' references its single horn and leonine body.
- **First use**: Earliest literary mentions appear in Han dynasty texts: Sima Xiangru's 'Rhapsody on the Imperial Park' and Wang Chong's Lunheng (c. 80 CE).

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Austroasiatic linguistic origin** _(Axel Schuessler)_

Linguist Axel Schuessler proposes the Chinese name 獬豸 derives from an Austroasiatic root related to words for 'deer' or 'stag' in Old Mon, Old Khmer, and Burmese, suggesting the myth may have originated with accounts of actual horned animals.

### Skeptical

**Judicial-political allegory** _(General scholarly consensus in folklore and legal history)_

The haetae/xiezhi is best understood as a symbolic construct designed to legitimate judicial authority and reinforce norms of official accountability, rather than a belief in a literal animal.
